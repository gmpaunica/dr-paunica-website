Conditions · Addictions

Care without judgment.

An addictive pattern is not a moral failure. Biology, learned reward, stress, environment and co-occurring mental health concerns can all shape why a behavior takes hold — and what helps it change.

Withdrawal can be a medical emergency.

Do not use either website for emergencies. For overdose, severe withdrawal, confusion, seizures, immediate danger or suicidal risk, call 112 now or go to the nearest emergency department. Do not stop alcohol or sedative medication abruptly without medical guidance.

In Belgium, call Zelfmoordlijn 1813 in Dutch or the Centre de Prévention du Suicide on 0800 32 123 in French. Both are free, anonymous and available 24/7.

You can call simply because things feel bad and you want someone to talk to.

Is this familiar?

The pattern may look like this.

You use or engage for longer, more often, or in greater amounts than you intended.

Attempts to cut back work briefly, then the pattern returns.

Craving, planning, using and recovering take up increasing space in your life.

Relationships, health, work or finances are affected, yet stopping still feels out of reach.

The pattern helps with sleep, anxiety, pain, loneliness or trauma — while creating problems of its own.

Alcohol, medication, drugs, screens and other repetitive reward-seeking behaviors can differ medically. A safe assessment identifies both the pattern and the risks specific to it.

How I approach assessment and treatment

Understand the function — then match the care.

First

A direct, respectful assessment

I ask about the pattern, withdrawal and overdose risk, physical health, medication and other substances. I also screen for depression, anxiety, trauma, ADHD, sleep problems and the context in which use developed.

Then

The right level of care

We consider your risks, strengths, supports and barriers. Some situations fit outpatient care; others require specialist addiction treatment, medically supervised withdrawal or a more intensive setting.

From there

Change that can be sustained

A plan may include motivational and psychotherapy-informed work, treatment of co-occurring concerns, medical options when appropriate, relapse-prevention planning and collaboration with specialist services.

When a consultation may help

A consultation may help when a substance or behavior is becoming harder to control, affecting daily life, carrying medical risk, or serving as the main way to cope with another concern.
