Conditions · Trauma & PTSD

Safety before speed.

Trauma work must be paced, safe and phase-oriented — never too much, too fast.

Not for emergencies.

Do not use either website for emergencies. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 112 now or go to the nearest emergency department.

In Belgium, call Zelfmoordlijn 1813 in Dutch or the Centre de Prévention du Suicide on 0800 32 123 in French. Both are free, anonymous and available 24/7.

You can call simply because things feel bad and you want someone to talk to.

Is this you?

It often looks like this.

Memories or nightmares return uninvited.

You avoid reminders — or feel strangely numb around them.

Part of you is always scanning; rest doesn't feel safe.

Shame, dissociation or difficulty trusting keep their distance from words.

The body stays braced, even when nothing is wrong.

Complex trauma may also show up as long-standing difficulty with trust, emotion regulation, self-worth, attachment and identity. Recognizing a few of these is not a diagnosis — it is a reason to look closer.

How treatment is paced

Safety before speed — always.

First

Stability and safety

Before anything is revisited, I help secure the ground: sleep, daily structure, body safety and enough support to stand on.

Then

Carefully paced processing

When indicated — and only then. I respect your capacity and keep the work within what is tolerable.

From there

Integration

We integrate the work back into relationships, identity and daily life. Where useful, I coordinate care with other professionals.

When a consultation may help

When past events keep shaping the present — through symptoms, relationships or the sense of safety — a careful, clinician-led assessment can establish what kind of support is appropriate now.
